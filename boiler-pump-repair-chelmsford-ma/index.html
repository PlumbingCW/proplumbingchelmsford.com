Boiler Pump Repair in Chelmsford, MA

The circulator pump moves hot water through your heating system. Here is how it fails, what you can check and when to call.

What the boiler pump does

A hot water boiler heats water, but the circulator pump is what moves it through the pipes to the radiators, baseboards or in-floor loops. If the boiler fires and the house stays cold, the pump is often the problem. A failed pump is one of the most common reasons a New England heating system quits in the middle of a cold snap.

This guide covers the signs of a failing pump, the safe checks a homeowner can make and when to bring in a plumber. If the house has no heat at all, see our boiler repair page for what to do right now.

Signs the pump is failing

  • The boiler runs and the radiators stay cold.
  • A grinding, humming or rattling noise from the pump housing.
  • The pump is hot to the touch but the water is not moving.
  • Water drips from the pump flanges or the seal.
  • Some zones heat and others do not.

A pump can also run and still do nothing. The motor spins, but the coupling has failed, so no water moves. Touching a pump that is hot but silent usually points to that.

Safe checks before you call

  1. Check the thermostatMake sure it is set to heat and is calling for it.
  2. Check the breaker and switchConfirm the boiler switch is on and the breaker has not tripped.
  3. Feel the pumpListen for a hum. Do not open the pump or touch wiring.
  4. Check the pressure gaugeMost systems run around 12 to 15 psi cold. Very low pressure can stop circulation.
  5. Call a plumberIf the pump is dead or noisy, do not keep running the boiler dry.

Do not bleed or drain the system unless you know the layout. Draining a closed system in winter risks freezing pipes and more damage.

Repair or replace

SituationUsual answer.
Noisy or seized pump on a working systemReplace the pump.
Leaking at the flanges onlyReplace flange gaskets.
Air in the lines, gurglingPurge air and check the air separator.
Pump fine, but still no heatCheck zone valves and the boiler.

Most circulators are replaced, not rebuilt. A new pump is a modest part, and the labor depends on how the old one is mounted. We confirm the model and flange spacing before the visit when we can.

Why pumps fail

Pumps run for months, then run harder when it gets very cold. Bearings wear, seals dry out and sludge builds in the system. Mineral buildup and dirty water shorten their life. A system that was never flushed is likely to lose pumps more often.

An annual service visit catches most of this. If the system is old, a plumbing installation visit to add isolation valves makes the next pump swap faster and neater.

Choosing a replacement circulator

Replacement pumps are not one size fits all. The new pump has to match the flange spacing, the voltage, the flow and the head the system needs. Fit the wrong one and the house heats unevenly or the pump runs loudly. Many plumbers carry a variable speed pump that adjusts to the demand, which uses less electricity and runs quieter than a fixed speed model.

What to ask before the visit

  • Which fuel does the boiler use, and how old is it?
  • Is the pump mounted with isolation valves, or will the system need partly draining?
  • Do all zones run from one pump or does each zone have its own?
  • Is the old pump labeled with a model number you can read to us?
  • Has the system been flushed or treated in recent years?

The answers shape the parts we bring and how long the job takes. Having the model number saves a second trip.
