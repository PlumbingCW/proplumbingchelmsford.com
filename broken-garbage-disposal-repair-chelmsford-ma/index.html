How to Repair a Broken Garbage Disposal in Chelmsford, MA

Humming, jammed, leaking or dead: what each symptom means and which fixes are safe to try before you call.

Call (855) 575-2890

Start with the symptom

A garbage disposal fails in only a few ways, and each one has a different fix. A hum with no spinning means a jam. Silence means no power or a tripped reset. A leak means a seal or a loose connection. Knowing which one you have saves time and often saves the disposal.

Before anything else, switch the disposal off at the wall and unplug it or turn off its breaker. Never put a hand in the disposal, even when it is off. Our garbage disposal page explains the service side. This guide helps you decide what to try yourself.

Match the symptom to the fix

SymptomLikely causeFirst step.
Humming, no spinJammed flywheelTurn the hex socket under the unit with a wrench.
Dead, no soundTripped reset or no powerPress the red reset button, check the breaker.
Leaks from the topSink flange sealTighten or replace the flange seal.
Leaks from the sideDishwasher hose or drainCheck the hose clamp and drain connection.
Leaks from the bottomCracked housingReplace the unit.
Drains slowlyClog in the trap or lineClear the trap and line.

Clearing a jam safely

  1. Power offUnplug the unit or shut off its breaker.
  2. Use the hex socketFit the wrench into the socket in the center of the underside and work it back and forth.
  3. Remove the objectUse tongs or pliers to pull out the object. Never use a hand.
  4. Press resetPress the red reset button on the underside.
  5. Test with waterRun cold water and switch the unit on.

If the unit jams again right away, the motor or the grind ring may be worn. That points to replacement.

Leaks and slow drains

A leak at the sink flange means the putty or gasket has aged. A leak at the dishwasher inlet is a loose or cracked hose. A leak from the housing itself is the end of the unit. These are fast jobs, but a mix of leaks and old fittings can turn into a full replacement.

A slow drain after the disposal is usually the trap or the branch line. Grease and ground food build up past the disposal outlet. Our drain cleaning service clears it, and a disposal that is repeatedly overloaded may be undersized for the household.

Repair or replace

  • Replace when the housing leaks, the motor is burned out or the unit is more than ten years old.
  • Repair when the problem is a jam, a seal, a hose or a reset.
  • Upgrade to a larger horsepower unit when the household cooks a lot.
  • Skip a disposal entirely if the home is not on a system that suits food waste.

We handle installs through garbage disposal installation and fit new units to standard sink openings.

Installing a new disposal: the sequence

  1. Disconnect the old unitShut off power, unhook the drain and dishwasher hose and twist the unit off its mounting ring.
  2. Replace the mounting assemblyRemove the sink flange, clean the opening and set a new flange with plumber's putty.
  3. Mount and wireHang the new unit, connect the cord or hardwire according to the manual.
  4. Connect the drain and hoseAttach the discharge pipe and the dishwasher inlet. Knock out the plug if the dishwasher connects here.
  5. TestRun water, switch it on and check every joint for drips.

The most common mistake is forgetting to knock out the dishwasher plug. The dishwasher then cannot drain and backs up into the sink.

The dishwasher connection

A dishwasher drains through a hose that rises to a high loop under the counter or connects to an air gap on the sink deck. The loop stops dirty sink water from siphoning back into the dishwasher. If the dishwasher drains into the disposal, the hose should not hang low and the knockout must be removed.

A dishwasher that leaves standing water after a cycle usually has a clogged hose, a missing knockout or a blocked disposal inlet. It is a quick check and a common reason for a service call.
