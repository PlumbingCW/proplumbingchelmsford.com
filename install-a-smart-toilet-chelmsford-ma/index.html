How to Install a Smart Toilet in Chelmsford, MA

What a smart toilet needs from your bathroom, the install steps and what to check before you buy.

Call (855) 575-2890

A smart toilet is a toilet plus a power cord

A smart toilet or electronic bidet seat adds heated seating, a spray wand, a dryer and sometimes an automatic lid. That brings in something a standard toilet never had, which is electricity. Most of the trouble in these installs comes from the outlet, the water connection and the space around the bowl.

This guide covers what to check before buying, what the install involves and where a plumber and an electrician each come in. For general toilet fixture work see plumbing installation.

Check these before you buy

  • An outlet within reach of the unit, protected by a GFCI.
  • A cold water supply with a shutoff behind the toilet.
  • Enough clearance behind and beside the bowl for the model.
  • Bowl shape: round or elongated, and a rough-in distance that matches.
  • A flush mechanism that fits, if the unit is a full toilet.

Rough-in is the distance from the wall to the center of the drain. The common size is twelve inches, but older houses vary. Measure before you order.

Seat or full toilet

OptionWhat it needsTypical job.
Bidet seat on your toiletOutlet and a T-fitting on the cold lineUnder an hour.

A bidet seat is the easy version. A full unit replaces the toilet, so it brings in the flange and wax ring work described in our toilet repair material.

Install steps for a full unit

  1. Shut off and remove the old toiletDrain it, disconnect the supply and lift it off the flange.
  2. Prepare the flangeCheck the flange height and condition, replace the seal and set new bolts.
  3. Set the new toiletLower the unit straight onto the flange and tighten evenly.
  4. Connect water and powerConnect the supply line and plug into a GFCI outlet.
  5. TestCheck all joints, then run through the unit's functions.

Manufacturers specify supply pressure. Most units want a normal household pressure and a clean line.

Who does which part

The plumber handles the toilet, the supply and the drain connection. A licensed electrician handles any new outlet or circuit. Do not run an extension cord to a toilet, and do not use a standard outlet near water.

If you are not sure whether your bathroom has a GFCI outlet, test the one you have. A wall outlet with a Test and Reset button is a GFCI. Without one, hire an electrician first.

Read the warranty and find out how easily the seat or the control board can be replaced. Parts availability matters more than a long feature list.

Water pressure, supply and the T-fitting

A bidet seat connects to the cold supply through a T-fitting between the shutoff valve and the toilet tank. The valve should be metal, not plastic, and should close fully. If the supply line is braided and old, replace it now while the connection is open. Water pressure that is too low weakens the spray, and pressure that is too high stresses the seals.

If the shutoff valve leaks or sticks, replace it first. A quarter-turn ball valve is a small upgrade that makes every later job easier.

Maintenance and troubleshooting

  • Wipe the nozzle and the seat with a soft cloth and mild cleaner.
  • Check the supply connection for drips after the first week.
  • Replace the water filter screen if the unit has one.
  • Reset the unit if the heater or nozzle stops working.
  • Call if the unit loses power repeatedly, which can mean a bad outlet.

Most problems come from a loose connection or an outlet that has tripped, not from the unit itself.

Choosing between a seat and a full unit

If your toilet bowl is sound and fits the seat, a bidet seat gives you most of the features at a fraction of the cost. A full smart toilet makes sense when the old toilet is due for replacement, when you want a clean, matching look or when the bathroom is small and a seat would crowd it. Compare the total cost, including electrical work, rather than only the sticker price.
