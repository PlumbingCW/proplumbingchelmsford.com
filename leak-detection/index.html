Leak detection in Chelmsford starts with the symptom

Few homeowners search for leak detection until something odd shows up. A water bill that has climbed without a reason, a meter that keeps turning with every fixture off, a patch of lawn that stays soggy, a stain growing on a ceiling. Our emergency leak detection service starts there. We ask what you are seeing, work out where the water is likely moving, and then confirm it with the right instrument.

If water is actively coming through a ceiling or pooling near the panel, shut off the main water valve, switch off power to the wet area if it is safe, and call our 24/7 line. We will send a plumber the same day when the schedule allows. If the leak is slow, a scheduled detection visit costs far less than a repair that starts with demolition.

Diagnosis comes first because most leak calls start without anyone knowing what is wrong. We tell you what we find, show you where, and give you the repair options before anyone picks up a saw.

Is your leak an emergency? Signs that call for action today

A leak is urgent when water is coming through a ceiling, when it is near electrical equipment, when the meter races with everything shut off, or when a basement floor is wet and nothing is spilling. It is less urgent but still worth a visit when a bill has crept up slowly or a faucet drip has become a stain.

Meter still running or a high water bill

Chelmsford is served by three separate water districts, the Chelmsford Water District, the North Chelmsford Water District and the East Chelmsford Water District. Each sets its own billing and meter practices, so the first step is to ask your district how to read your meter and whether it can check for unusual use.

A simple test narrows things down. With every tap, appliance and irrigation line off, note the meter reading, wait twenty minutes, then read it again. A change points to a leak between the meter and the fixtures, a running toilet or a failed water heater valve. A toilet repair may be all you need, and we will tell you if it is.

How we find a leak without opening the walls

  1. Listen and gatherWe ask where, when and what you have seen, and check fixtures, valves and appliance hookups first.
  2. Pressure and meter testWe isolate sections of the system to see which leg is losing water.
  3. Acoustic and thermal toolsListening gear picks up the sound of water escaping, and a thermal camera shows cold or warm spots behind finishes.
  4. Mark and confirmWe mark the spot so the repair opens the smallest possible area.
  5. Explain the optionsYou get the repair choices and costs before any work begins.

No tool finds every leak. Water travels along framing and pipe, so the stain is often not where the leak is. We confirm with at least two methods before marking a spot.

Ceiling, wall, crawl space and under-slab leaks

Ceiling stains below a bathroom usually trace to a shower pan, a supply fitting or a drain joint. Wall leaks often come from a failed supply valve or a hose bib. Leaks under a basement slab or in the yard are rarer and harder to reach, and they call for a different tool and often a different budget.

Buried service lines between the street and the house can leak without any visible sign. If the lawn is soggy and the meter is moving, we will walk the line and give you the repair options, which may include repiping the run rather than patching it. For lines that have already burst, see our burst pipe repair page.
