How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Chelmsford, MA

A simple meter test that tells you whether water is leaking, and what to do when it is.

Call (888) 910-8644

Is water leaking when no one is using it?

Most hidden leaks give no sign until the bill arrives or a ceiling stain appears. The water meter is the quickest way to know. If it moves when every tap and appliance in the house is off, water is going somewhere. That test costs nothing and takes ten minutes.

This guide walks you through the test, explains what the result means and shows how a plumber finds the leak without tearing out walls. Our leak detection page covers the service itself.

The ten-minute meter test

  1. Stop all water useTurn off every tap, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the ice maker. Make sure no one flushes.
  2. Find the meterIt is usually in the basement near the main shutoff, or in a pit outside.
  3. Read the dialNote the position of the small leak indicator, a triangle or a small wheel, and the number.
  4. Wait ten minutesDo not use water during this time.
  5. Read it againIf the indicator moved or the number changed, you have a leak.

A moving meter does not tell you where. It only tells you that the leak is real.

Narrowing down where the leak is

Close the main shutoff. If the meter stops moving once the valve is closed, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the valve. If the meter does not move at all with the valve open and nothing running, the leak is gone or intermittent.

The usual suspects are a running toilet, a dripping hot water line, a failing water heater relief valve and a pipe in a wall or under the floor. A toilet is the most common. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank. If color reaches the bowl without flushing, the flapper leaks.

What happens after the leak is found

  • Small fitting leak: tighten or replace the fitting.
  • Pinhole in copper: cut out and sleeve the section.
  • Failed supply line: replace the line.
  • Widespread pinholes: consider repiping.

If a pipe has already burst, shut the main and go to our burst pipe repair page.

Why a small leak costs more than you expect

A faucet that drips once a second wastes several gallons a day. A toilet that leaks silently can waste far more. Over a billing period that adds up to a noticeable bill, and the water also damages wood, drywall and insulation. Mold follows a persistent wet spot within days in a warm, closed space.

Fixing a small leak early costs a fraction of the repair after the damage spreads. If the meter test says water is moving, act on it.

What to do if you find the leak

  • Shut off the nearest fixture valve, or the main if you cannot find it.
  • Photograph the damage for your records and for your insurer.
  • Dry wet materials quickly with fans and a dehumidifier.
  • Call a plumber before opening walls, so the leak is located first.
  • Ask your water district about billing adjustments after a documented leak.

Districts differ on whether they adjust a bill after a repaired leak, so ask yours rather than assuming.

Local notes for Chelmsford homes

Chelmsford has three separate water systems, the Chelmsford Water District, the North Chelmsford Water District and the East Chelmsford Water District. Call the district that bills you about the meter itself, billing questions and anything on the street side. A leak on your side of the meter is yours to fix, and we can find it.

Do not guess about a high bill. Ask your district whether it reads the meter automatically and how often. A bill that doubles with no change in use is a good reason to run the test above. For help in nearby towns see Westford and Lowell.

Water suppliers are required to publish an annual water quality report under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act, and your district can explain its metering and billing guidelines.
