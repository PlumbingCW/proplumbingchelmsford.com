Repiping a Chelmsford home: when and how

Repiping replaces the water supply pipes in a house. It is not an emergency service, it is a decision, and it usually follows a pattern: a pinhole leak, then another, then low pressure and discolored water. Our repiping service in Chelmsford covers partial runs and whole houses, in PEX or copper.

Pipes fail from the inside out. Galvanized steel rusts and narrows, which shows up as falling pressure and brown water. Copper can develop pinholes depending on water chemistry. One leak is a repair. Several, in different places, are a trend.

If a pipe is leaking right now, start with our burst pipe repair page. This page is for planning the replacement.

Signs it is time to repipe

  • Repeated leaks in different parts of the house.
  • Brown or rusty water when you first open a tap.
  • Pressure that has dropped across the house, not at one fixture.
  • Galvanized pipe that is original to an older home.
  • A plumber has told you the pipe is thin or corroded.

PEX or copper: choosing a material

MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs.
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, handles freezing betterNot for sunlight exposure, needs proper support.
CopperLong track record, rigid, heat tolerantHigher material cost, can pinhole in some water.
CPVCLower cost, rigidBrittle with age, less common now.

Material choice depends on the house, the water and your budget. We explain the options in plain terms, and the licensed plumber doing the work pulls the permit.

Whole-house or partial repipe

A partial repipe replaces the worst runs, such as the kitchen and bath on one floor or a stretch in the basement. A whole-house repipe replaces everything and is usually more efficient if more than half the system is suspect.

Planning matters. Walls need access holes, and we patch what we open. We schedule water shutoffs for set hours so you know when the house is without water.

How a repipe unfolds

  1. Walk-throughWe trace the system, find shutoffs and note where walls need access.
  2. Plan and permitYou approve the plan and the licensed plumber handles the permit.
  3. Replace the runsWe install new supply lines and fixtures connections.
  4. Pressure test and inspectThe town's plumbing inspector reviews the work.
  5. Patch and clean upWe close access holes and leave the work area tidy.

Living through a repipe

A repipe is a few days of noise and dust, not weeks. The water is shut off during the work, so plan for no running water during the day and a restored supply by evening whenever possible. We protect floors and furniture, open small access holes where we need them and patch what we open.

  • Move furniture away from walls where we expect to cut.
  • Plan for no water at certain hours and fill a few jugs beforehand.
  • Tell us about pets and where they will stay.
  • Ask us which holes will be patched and which will be left for a drywall finisher.

When the job is done, we flush the lines, test for leaks and show you where the shutoffs are. If you have a question later about a valve or a line, call.
