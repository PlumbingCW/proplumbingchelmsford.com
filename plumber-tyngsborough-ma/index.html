Winter, thaw and the Merrimack: Tyngsborough's plumbing year

Tyngsborough is a town of about 12,400 people on the Merrimack River, split in two by the river, with the Tyngsborough Bridge connecting the sides. It lies on the Route 3 corridor on the New Hampshire line, roughly seven miles north of Chelmsford. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock.

Winter is the main event. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages and crawl spaces freeze first, and the break often shows up on the first warm afternoon. Spring brings its own trouble: meltwater into basements and sump pumps that have not run since fall.

Winter: frozen pipes and bursts

A faucet that runs a trickle or stops during a cold snap is a warning. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls, let a tap drip and call before the pipe splits. If a pipe has burst, shut the main and see burst pipe repair.

Spring thaw, sump pumps and river-valley basements

Homes near the Merrimack and the town's lakes and ponds can see groundwater rise quickly. Test your sump pump in early spring by pouring water into the pit. If it does not start, sump pump service gets it running. A battery backup is smart for outages.

Drought has the opposite effect on wells. The town's well guidance warns that wells can drop in dry spells, so conserve water and watch for sediment.

Wells, septic and what your plumber can do

Some Tyngsborough homes use private wells and septic systems. The town's guidance covers testing for arsenic, uranium, radon, lead and PFAS, and the state offers septic repair loans and a Title 5 tax credit for qualifying homeowners. Wells and tanks belong to specialists. Everything inside the house, from fixtures and treatment hookups to a water heater, is ours.

If your drains are slow and you are on septic, tell us before we run a cable. A full tank is not a clog.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Tyngsborough?

Yes. Tyngsborough uses ZIP 01879, which covers the whole town on both sides of the Merrimack River, from the Route 3 corridor to the lake neighborhoods and the New Hampshire line.

What to do while we are on the way to Tyngsborough

  • Shut off the main valve if water is escaping, and shut off power to wet areas if it is safe.
  • Stop water use for a drain backup.
  • If your home is on a well, turn off the well pump when a pipe has burst.
  • Tell us if the home is on town water or a private well.
  • Keep a path clear to the basement or utility area.

A private well changes the call. A burst pipe on a well system keeps pumping until the pump is shut off, and a flooded pump house is a separate problem. Tell dispatch which system you have.
