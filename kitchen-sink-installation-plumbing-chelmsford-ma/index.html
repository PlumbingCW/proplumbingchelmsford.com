The plumbing under a new kitchen sink

Dropping in a new sink is mostly a carpentry and countertop job. The plumbing underneath is what decides whether the cabinet stays dry. A sink swap touches the faucet supply lines, the shutoff valves, the drain basket, the tailpiece and the trap. Each of those joints can leak if it is rushed.

This guide covers what a sink installation involves, what to replace instead of reuse and when to call a plumber. If the faucet is the only problem, our faucet repair page may be all you need. For a larger kitchen job see plumbing installation.

What to replace and what to reuse

PartReuse?Why.
Faucet supply linesReplaceOld braided lines can burst and are cheap to replace.
Shutoff valvesReplace if stiff or leakingA valve that will not close defeats the swap.
Drain basket and tailpieceReplaceNew sinks seldom match old fittings.
P-trapReplace if cracked or corrodedCheap and easy while open.
DisposalReuse if soundCheck the flange fit.

The shutoff valves are the part people regret skipping. If they do not close fully, you cannot test the new connections without flooding the cabinet.

Sequence of the job

  1. Shut off the waterClose the angle stops under the sink. If they do not close, shut the main.
  2. Disconnect and removeRelease the supply lines, the trap and the disposal. Cut any mounting clips and lift out the sink.
  3. Test fit the new sinkDry fit the sink and mark the faucet and drain locations.
  4. Install the faucet and drainFit the faucet and basket before the sink goes in, because the space beneath is tight.
  5. Connect and testReconnect supplies and trap. Run water, fill the basin, drain it and check every joint with a dry paper towel.

Mistakes that cause leaks

  • Overtightening plastic nuts, which cracks the threads.
  • Reusing a worn washer or a compressed trap gasket.
  • A trap that slopes the wrong way, so water sits and weeps.
  • Skipping plumber's putty or silicone at the drain flange.
  • Testing with the drain only half filled.

A slow drip under a cabinet rots the floor of the cabinet and can run into the wall. Test with a dry tissue held under each joint after the first fill and again the next morning.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber when the shutoffs are seized, the drain pipe is corroded, the layout needs to move or you are adding a dishwasher or disposal. Moving a drain or a supply line is a change to the plumbing system, and in Massachusetts that is work for a licensed plumber.

Our garbage disposal page covers the disposal connection if one is part of the project.

Sink styles and what they change underneath

Sink stylePlumbing impact.
Drop-inSimple, standard drain placement.
UndermountNeeds clips, silicone and countertop support.
Farmhouse apronNeeds a cabinet cutout and often a deeper trap.
Double bowlNeeds a continuous waste or a branch tailpiece.
Single large bowlSimplest drain, but a disposal may crowd the cabinet.

Plan the drain layout before you choose. A deep bowl pushes the trap lower, and the trap still has to slope into the wall.

Testing for leaks after the swap

Fill the basin completely and let it drain while you watch each joint. Run the disposal if one is installed. Check again after an hour and again the next day. A joint that weeps a drop every few minutes will not look like much, but it rots a cabinet floor in months.

Tools and parts to have on hand

  • Adjustable wrench, channel locks and a basin wrench for faucet nuts.
  • Plumber's putty, silicone and thread tape.
  • New supply lines and a trap kit.
  • A bucket, towels and a headlamp.
  • A utility knife to cut old caulk.

Having the right parts before you shut off the water saves a trip to the store with the kitchen out of service. Many sink swaps stretch from two hours to an afternoon because a part was missing.

Local notes for Chelmsford homes

In Massachusetts only licensed plumbers may do plumbing work, and replacing or relocating fixtures, valves or piping needs a permit through the local plumbing inspector. Minor repairs, such as clearing a blocked drain or fixing a leaky faucet, do not. Ask the town's inspector whether a like-for-like sink swap in your house falls under a permit.

Homes in the older parts of Chelmsford, such as the area around the town center, may have older shutoff valves and galvanized or copper lines under the sink. Check those before assuming the new sink will connect cleanly. For more help nearby see Westford and Billerica.
