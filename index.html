Chelmsford, village by village

Chelmsford has several distinct villages: the town center around the Chelmsford Center Historic District, North Chelmsford, South Chelmsford, West Chelmsford and East Chelmsford. Central Square is where Routes 4 and 110 meet, and I-495 and Route 3 cross in the town. Those roads matter to a dispatcher, because they set the route to your door at 2 a.m.

The town was incorporated in 1655, and the Middlesex Canal passed through it in the early 1800s. Older homes near the center and the villages often have older plumbing, which is why we ask the age of the house on every call. A house with original galvanized or failing copper supply lines is a candidate for repiping, while a newer home with a leak is usually a fitting.

Water comes from three separate districts, and sewer service is run by the town's Sewer Division, which reports 190 miles of public sewer line and 41 pump stations. Knowing which utility serves your street saves time during an emergency.

How to check any plumber before work starts

In Massachusetts only a licensed master or journeyman plumber can do plumbing work in your home, and only with a permit from the local plumbing inspector. Ask any plumber for proof, including the plumber you reach through this site. The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters lets you check licensure and discipline history, and the license type and number must appear on a plumber's advertising.

Minor repairs, such as clearing a blocked drain or fixing a leaky faucet, do not need a permit. Replacing or relocating fixtures, valves or piping does. Ask to see the permit, because it shows the work will be inspected.
