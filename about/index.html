Who answers the phone

Chelmsford Pro Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing dispatch service for Chelmsford, Massachusetts and four nearby towns. We take your call, work out what is wrong, and arrange a plumber. We keep our claims to what we can stand behind, which is why you will not find invented review counts, awards or founding dates on this site.

This website is a call line, not a plumbing contractor. The plumber who does the work holds the license, pulls the permit as Massachusetts requires and carries the insurance, and the town does the inspection. Ask for the license type and number before work starts.

How to check any plumber in Massachusetts

Only licensed master or journeyman plumbers may do plumbing work in a Massachusetts home. The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters keeps license records, and a license type and number must appear on a plumber's advertising. Ask for the permit on any job that replaces or relocates piping, because it shows the work will be inspected.

Minor repairs, such as clearing a blocked drain or fixing a leaky faucet, do not need a permit. Replacing or relocating fixtures, valves or piping does. If a plumber tells you a permit is not needed for a larger job, ask the town's plumbing inspector.

Pages for each service list what drives the price, such as access, materials, the time of day and whether a permit is needed. You can read those before you call.

Safety information we give on every call

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 before you call us.
  • If water is near the electrical panel, stay clear and tell us when you call.
  • Shut off the main water valve if a pipe has burst and know where it is.
  • Do not use drain chemicals on a clog you plan to have cleared, because they are hazardous to the plumber who opens the line.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing water that may contain sewage.

These steps cost nothing and they reduce damage while a plumber is on the way.

Seasonal plumbing in a New England town

Plumbing calls follow the weather. Winter brings frozen pipes, burst lines and heating outages. Spring brings thaw, high groundwater and sump pump failures. Summer brings heavy use of drains, outdoor spigots and water heaters. Fall is the right time to service boilers and shut off outdoor lines.

Knowing this helps you plan. A boiler tune-up in October costs less than a midnight call in January, and testing a sump pump in March beats discovering it failed in the middle of a storm.
