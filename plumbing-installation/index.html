Plumbing installation for Chelmsford remodels and additions

Plumbing installation covers work that adds or changes piping: a new bathroom, a relocated kitchen sink, a laundry hookup, a basement bathroom, an outdoor spigot. In Chelmsford these projects come with a permit, because Massachusetts requires one when fixtures or piping are replaced or relocated.

Good installation starts before drywall. We review the drawings or walk the space, locate the supply and drain paths, and plan venting. A drain without proper slope or venting causes gurgling and slow flow no matter how nice the fixture.

If your project replaces a toilet, sink or tub, see toilet repair and installation or faucet and sink repair.

Permits, inspections and the licensed plumber

Only a licensed master or journeyman plumber can do plumbing work in a Massachusetts home, and only with a permit from the local plumbing inspector. Permits are issued to licensed plumbers, not to homeowners. Minor repairs such as a leaky faucet do not require one, while replacing or relocating fixtures and piping does.

You can verify a plumber's license through the state board. A licensee's license type and number must appear on any sign, listing or advertisement. Ask to see the permit, which shows the work will be inspected.

Basement and below-grade bathrooms

A basement bathroom often sits below the sewer line and needs an ejector pump or a sewage basin. That raises the stakes for venting and for what goes in the basin. We plan the basin, the pump and the vent together.

Related work such as sump pump service is separate. Do not drain a sump into the sewer.

Planning rough-in before the walls close

The cheapest time to change plumbing is before drywall goes up. A rough-in sets the supply lines, drains and vents inside the walls and floor. Mistakes after closing mean cutting open finished surfaces. We walk the space with you, mark fixture centers and confirm clearances before we run pipe.

Rough-in itemWhat to confirm.
Toilet drainDistance from the finished wall to the drain center.
Vanity suppliesHeight and spacing for the faucet you chose.
Shower valveDepth for the trim kit and wall thickness.
Laundry boxLocation of the drain and shutoffs.
VentA clear path to the roof or an approved air admittance valve.

Bring the fixture spec sheets. A faucet or valve with unusual dimensions is easier to plan for than to fix later.

Choosing pipe and fittings

Most new supply work uses PEX or copper. PEX is flexible, quicker to run and resists bursting better in a freeze, while copper is rigid and long established. Drains and vents use PVC or ABS depending on what the code and the existing system accept. The choice depends on the house, the budget and what the inspector will pass.

We explain what we plan to use and why before work begins, and we use fittings that match what is already in the house so you do not mix materials that react badly.
