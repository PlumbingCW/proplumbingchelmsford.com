Basement waterproofing and water control in Chelmsford

A damp basement is not always a waterproofing problem. Water can come from the ground, from a leaking pipe, from a failed sump pump or from a drain backup. We start by finding which, because the fix differs. Our basement waterproofing service in Chelmsford focuses on the plumbing side: sump systems, interior drainage tied to a sump, leak detection and drain checks.

If water is coming in during a storm, see our emergency sump pump page. This page is for the planned work after the immediate problem is handled.

We are straightforward about scope. Foundation crack injection, exterior excavation and regrading are specialty work, and we say when you need one.

Planning a drainage system

An interior perimeter drain collects water at the floor edge and sends it to a sump. It protects against hydrostatic pressure but does not stop moisture from walls. It needs a pump that will not fail, so a backup pairs with it.

Surface water also matters. Gutters, downspouts and grading push water to the foundation, and fixing those costs less than any drain.

Interior drains versus exterior work

Water control around a foundation comes in two families. Exterior work, such as regrading, gutters and excavation to the footing, stops water before it reaches the wall. Interior work collects water that has already arrived and sends it to a sump pit. Each has a place, and the plumbing side of the job is the interior system.

ApproachBest forLimit.
Regrade and extend downspoutsSurface water near the wallDoes nothing for a high water table.
Interior perimeter drain to a sumpWater entering at the floor and wall jointNeeds a working pump and discharge.
Exterior excavation and membraneWall seepage and a failing coatingCostly, heavy equipment, landscaping repair.
Crack injectionA single steady crackNot a fix for hydrostatic pressure overall.

We handle the interior drain, the pit, the pump and the discharge. If your wall is bowing or cracked wide, a foundation specialist or engineer should look first, and we will say so plainly. Our sump pump page covers the pump side in more detail.

Questions to ask any contractor before you sign

  • Where will the collected water go once it reaches the pump?
  • What happens to the system when the power fails?
  • Who pulls the permit if one is required?
  • Is the work warranted in writing, and what does the warranty exclude?
  • Will a licensed plumber do the plumbing connections?

A good answer to each is specific and short. A vague answer to the discharge question is the most common warning sign, because a system that moves water to a spot where it flows back to the foundation just repeats the problem.
