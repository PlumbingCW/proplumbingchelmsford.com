An old farm village, and one question to settle first

South Chelmsford began as a farming village. The town master plan describes it as a small historic village along Route 27 and Maple Road, and the Chelmsford Historical Society counts it among the five original villages.

For a plumbing call, the most useful thing to know about a house here is what it drains to. That answer is not the same on every street, and it decides which service fits. This page explains how to find out and what we could verify about the area.

What the records say about South Chelmsford

  • The 1980 state survey says South Chelmsford developed primarily as an agricultural village in the Federal period, with a garrison house dating from 1690 and farms around Heart Pond.
  • It records a Baptist meetinghouse built in 1836 and Liberty Hall, an Italianate public hall built around 1870.
  • The same survey notes high-style Federal houses in the vicinity, and the master plan describes the village as small and historic.

Find out what your house drains to

Chelmsford has a public sewer system, and it has homes on septic systems. The town's pages do not list which streets in South Chelmsford fall on which side, and we will not guess. Two calls settle it. The Sewer Division, at 978-250-5233, keeps as-built plans for most sewer connections. The Board of Health handles septic systems.

If you are on septic, the town's On-Site Wastewater System Maintenance and Inspection regulation, adopted February 3, 2026, sets how often systems are pumped and inspected. The schedule depends on the system's age and on whether the property is in a High or Non-High Aquifer Area, which the town decides from its GIS map. A septic property with a garbage disposal must be pumped every year.

When a slow drain is a septic question

If every drain in the house is slow, not just one, the problem may be the tank or the leach field and not a clog. Running a cable through the house line will not fix that. Tell us you are on septic when you call, and we will say whether the work is ours or a septic contractor's.

If the drain is slow at one fixture only, it is usually local to that fixture, and drain cleaning fits. If a main line on sewer keeps backing up, a camera inspection shows why before anyone digs.

Ponds, older houses and the questions to ask

The master plan notes that homes around the town's lakes and ponds reflect a one-time vacation area. It does not say which houses began as seasonal cottages, and we do not assume it. Ask the age of your house and of the water heater, and look for the main shutoff before you need it.

Houses of different ages can have sound pipe or bad pipe, so we inspect first. If a line has failed, sewer line repair and repiping are explained on their own pages.

Which service fits the problem

  • One slow fixture: drain cleaning.
  • Every drain slow, or a backup: sewer line cleaning, after you confirm sewer or septic.
  • Water heater failure: repair or replacement.
  • A basement that takes on water: sump pump service.

What we could not confirm

We could not confirm a published boundary for South Chelmsford, which streets are sewered, or which water district serves each street. We found no evidence that plumbing problems are more common here than elsewhere in town, so this page makes no such claim.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts follows the state plumbing code, 248 CMR. Septic systems follow Title 5, 310 CMR 15.000, and Chelmsford's Board of Health Article XIX (sections 201-151 to 201-158) sets local pumping and inspection schedules, the local standard for septic care.
