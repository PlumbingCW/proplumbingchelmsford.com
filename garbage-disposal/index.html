Garbage disposal repair and installation in Chelmsford

A garbage disposal is one of the hardest-working appliances under the sink. When it jams, leaks or stops, the kitchen suffers. Our emergency garbage disposal repair service in Chelmsford runs 24/7. We repair what is repairable, replace what is not and reconnect the dishwasher drain properly.

If your disposal hums but does not turn, turn it off and do not put a hand inside. If it is leaking, turn off the breaker, place a bucket and call. Never use chemical drain cleaners in a disposal, because they can damage seals and the next person who works on it.

Disposals tied to a clogged sink drain are a combination issue. We clear the line as well as fixing the unit.

Leaking disposal: urgent or can it wait

Water under the sink is urgent if it is more than a drip. Disposals leak at the sink flange, the dishwasher inlet, the drain outlet or through the body. A body leak means the unit is done.

What to do before you reach into a disposal

  • Turn the switch off, then unplug the unit or turn off its breaker.
  • Never put a hand or fingers into the chamber, even when it is off.
  • Use tongs or pliers to remove an object.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaner into a unit you plan to have repaired.
  • Call us when the unit smokes, smells of burning or leaks near the wiring.

Installing or replacing a disposal

We install standard and continuous-feed models, reconnect the dishwasher drain with a proper high loop or air gap and test for leaks. Tell us if you want a quieter or larger-horsepower unit.

When the sink drain is slow, we check the line before installing a new unit, since a clog downstream defeats a good disposal. See drain cleaning for more.

Habits that protect a disposal

  • Run cold water before, during and after use.
  • Skip grease, coffee grounds in volume, fibrous peels and bones.
  • Cut large scraps and feed gradually.
  • Clean with ice or citrus instead of chemicals.

Choosing a disposal that matches your kitchen

Disposals come in three common horsepower ranges. A smaller motor suits a household that rarely cooks. A mid-range unit suits most families. A larger motor handles heavy use and tougher scraps. A bigger motor also costs more, weighs more and takes up more room under the sink.

FeatureWhy it matters.
HorsepowerSets how well it handles tougher scraps.
Continuous feed or batch feedBatch feed runs only with the stopper in place.
Stainless grinding partsResist corrosion and last longer.
Sound insulationNoticeable in an open kitchen.
Dishwasher inletNeeded if the dishwasher drains through it.

Check how much clearance sits under your sink before buying. A large unit and a deep trap may not fit with a deep basin. We measure first and recommend a unit that fits.

Habits that keep a disposal running

  • Run cold water before, during and for several seconds after grinding.
  • Feed scraps in small amounts rather than a full load.
  • Keep grease, bones, pits and fibrous peels out.
  • Grind a few ice cubes now and then to scrub the chamber.
  • Never pour chemical cleaner into a disposal.

Most failures we see come from jams and overloaded lines rather than worn motors.
