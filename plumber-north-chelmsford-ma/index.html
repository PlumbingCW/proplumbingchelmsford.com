Plumbing help in a village with its own ZIP and water district

North Chelmsford is one of the five original villages of Chelmsford, and it has the most separate identity of them. It has its own ZIP code, 01863, its own post office and library, and its own water district. The village centers on Vinal Square, where Routes 3A and 40 meet, and Freeman Lake sits along Route 40 close to the square.

This page is for people in that village who want to know how a plumbing call works here. We cover the local facts we could verify, say plainly what we could not, and point you to the service page that matches your problem.

What we could verify about North Chelmsford

  • North Chelmsford has the ZIP code 01863. The rest of Chelmsford uses 01824.
  • Vinal Square, at the meeting of Routes 3A and 40, is the village's historic center, and the town master plan describes a small cluster of businesses around it.
  • Freeman Lake, formerly Newfield Pond and also called Crystal Lake, lies along Route 40 near the square. It was renamed in 1986.
  • A 1980 state survey by the Massachusetts Historical Commission calls North Chelmsford the town's largest village by the early industrial era, built up around water-powered textile mills along the Merrimack River side of town.

Water: this village has its own district

Chelmsford has three water districts: the Chelmsford Water District, the North Chelmsford Water District and the East Chelmsford Water District. North Chelmsford has its own, so questions about a bill, usage or water quality go to that district and not to the town offices. Your water bill shows which district serves you.

The line between the district's meter and your house belongs to you. A bill that jumps after a quiet month is a reason to check the meter before calling anyone, and the test is simple: turn every tap and appliance off and watch whether the meter still moves. If it does, leak detection is the next call.

Older village streets and what to ask about them

The same 1980 survey describes modest side-hall houses and cottages built in the late 1800s on side streets near Groton Road and Middlesex Street, and larger houses on Middlesex Street itself. That tells you the village has housing from several eras. It does not tell you what is inside any one wall.

A house's age is a reason to ask questions, not proof of a problem. Know where your main shutoff is, note what year the water heater was installed, and tell us the age of the house when you call. A plumber should look at the pipes before recommending repiping, not after.

Sewer or septic: confirm before you book

The town Sewer Division runs the public system, which its own page puts at 190 miles of sewer line and 41 pump stations. That page does not say which streets in North Chelmsford are sewered, and neither can we. The Sewer Division keeps as-built plans for most connections, though it does not mark lines on private property. Its office number is 978-250-5233.

If your house is on septic, a town Board of Health regulation adopted on February 3, 2026 sets pumping and inspection schedules, and a septic property with a garbage disposal must be pumped every year. Septic work is a job for a septic contractor, and we will say so on the phone.

Which service fits the problem

  • Slow or backed-up drains: drain cleaning, or hydro jetting for grease and scale in a line.
  • More than one fixture backing up, or gurgling at the lowest drain: sewer line cleaning and a sewer camera inspection.
  • Rusty water, no hot water or a leaking tank: water heater repair or water heater replacement.
  • Water coming from a wall or ceiling: shut off the main and see burst pipe repair.

What we could not confirm

The town does not publish a boundary for the village, so we use Vinal Square, Freeman Lake and the 01863 ZIP code as anchors. We could not confirm which streets are on sewer, which are on septic, or how old the plumbing is on any given street. We have no data showing that plumbing fails more often in North Chelmsford than elsewhere, and this page does not claim it.

Plumbing work in Massachusetts follows the state plumbing code, 248 CMR, administered by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters. Septic systems follow Title 5, 310 CMR 15.000, and the Chelmsford Board of Health regulation adopted February 3, 2026 adds local pumping and inspection schedules, which is the standard for septic care in town. Village details come from the Massachusetts Historical Commission's 1980 reconnaissance survey of Chelmsford and the town's water district and sewer pages.
