Toilet repair and installation in Chelmsford

A toilet that will not stop running wastes water. One that overflows or leaks at the base is an emergency. Our emergency toilet repair service in Chelmsford runs 24/7, and we also install new toilets when yours is cracked or beyond repair.

If a toilet is overflowing, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. Mop up quickly and keep the area dry. If the water is dirty, keep children and pets away. Then call.

Many toilet calls are simple parts: a flapper, a fill valve, a wax ring. We fix those fast. Others are a bigger sign, like a crack in the bowl or a leak under the floor.

What to do when a toilet overflows

  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper closed, or close the supply valve behind the toilet.
  • Do not flush again.
  • Put towels down and keep children away from the water.
  • Wear gloves if the water is dirty.
  • Call us if more than one fixture is affected.

Leaking at the base

Water at the base of a toilet can come from a failed wax ring, loose closet bolts, a cracked bowl or a leaking supply. A wax ring that has failed lets water seep into the subfloor. Soft flooring and a musty smell are warning signs.

We lift the toilet, inspect the flange, replace the wax ring or the flange as needed and check the floor. If the leak has been going a while, a leak detection visit may be a sensible follow-up.

The running toilet checklist

  1. Lift the tank lidWatch what happens after a flush.
  2. Check the flapperIf it does not seal, water leaks into the bowl.
  3. Check the fill valveIf it does not shut off, water spills down the overflow tube.
  4. Check the chainToo tight or too loose, it keeps the flapper from seating.
  5. Test with food coloringColor in the bowl without a flush confirms a flapper leak.

Most running toilets are a flapper or a fill valve. Both are inexpensive parts, and both can be swapped in a few minutes.

Clogs that keep returning

A toilet that clogs repeatedly may have a partial blockage in the trap, a low-flow design that cannot move waste or a vent problem that slows drainage. A plunger clears the first two for a while. If a toilet gurgles, or a nearby tub or sink also drains slowly, the issue is deeper than the bowl. See our drain cleaning page for the line itself.
