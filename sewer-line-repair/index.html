Sewer line repair in Chelmsford: from backup to fix

A sewer line repair starts with a diagnosis. A line that backs up once might be a clog. A line that backs up again and again, gurgles, or leaves wet patches in the yard usually has a defect. Our emergency sewer line repair service is available 24/7 in Chelmsford, and we begin with a camera so the repair addresses the real problem.

If sewage is coming up in the house, stop using water, keep people clear and call. If you see an overflow in the yard, tell us. The town's Sewer Division handles the public main, and its sewer emergency number is 978-250-5233.

Many repair calls describe a localized crack with roots entering through it. That is a spot repair candidate, not a whole-line replacement, and we say so when the camera supports it.

Sewage in a living space is both an inconvenience and a health risk. Wear gloves and boots if you must enter, keep children and pets away and avoid mixing cleaners.

Common reasons a sewer line fails

  • Roots working into joints and cracks, then growing into a mat that traps waste.
  • Pipe that has shifted, offset or sagged, leaving a low spot that holds water.
  • Aging materials such as clay, cast iron or Orangeburg that cracks or deforms.
  • Damage during excavation or from heavy equipment over the line.
  • Collapsed sections from soil movement and age.

Who owns what: your lateral and the town main

In Chelmsford, the town reports 190 miles of public sewer line and 41 pump stations. The public main is the town's responsibility, and the lateral that connects your house to it is usually yours. If a blockage sits on the town side, call the Sewer Division. If it sits on yours, we repair it.

Permits matter. In Massachusetts, only licensed plumbers pull plumbing permits, and replaced piping needs one. Ask the Chelmsford building department what applies. Our sewer inspection service gives you video to take to the town if needed.
