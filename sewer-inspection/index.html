Sewer camera inspection in Chelmsford

Sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a push rod and sends it through the line from a cleanout. It records cracks, offsets, roots, belly sections and the pipe material. A scheduled inspection in Chelmsford is a sensible step when a line keeps clogging, when you are buying a house, or when a repair estimate calls for work you cannot see.

The camera also locates the line. A transmitter on the camera head lets us mark the pipe's path and depth at the surface, which matters before any digging or landscaping.

If water is already backing up, see our emergency sewer line cleaning page. This page is for planned inspections.

What the camera finds

  • Root intrusion at joints and cracks.
  • Cracks, breaks and collapsed sections.
  • Offsets where pipe sections have shifted.
  • Belly sections where water pools.
  • Pipe material and connections to the public main.
  • Grease or scale that jetting could remove.

Villages such as North Chelmsford have their own water district, and the town's Sewer Division can say whether your street is sewered.

Why inspect before you buy a house

A standard home inspection does not usually include the sewer lateral. A lateral in poor condition can cost far more than a faucet or a water heater. A camera tells you before closing and gives you a basis to negotiate or plan.

Homes of many ages sit side by side in Chelmsford, and the lateral is out of sight in all of them. A video record is the only way to see it.

How an inspection works

  1. Locate the cleanoutIf none exists, we access the line through a fixture or roof vent.
  2. Run the cameraWe send the camera through the line while recording.
  3. Mark the lineWe locate defects and depth at the surface if needed.
  4. Review the footageWe show you the video and explain what matters.

What the camera video shows

The video is the most useful record of a sewer line you can have. It shows pipe material, joints, root intrusion, cracks, low spots where water pools and the location of any problem. We note the distance from the cleanout so a repair, if needed, goes to the right place.

What you see on cameraWhat it usually means.
Tiny root hairs at a jointA joint is loose enough to let in moisture.
Standing water for a lengthA sag or belly where debris collects.
Flaking, ridged cast ironScale buildup or corrosion.
Offset or separated jointSoil movement, a repair candidate.
Clean pipe, no defectsThe clog was a one-time event.

When an inspection is worth doing

  • Before you buy a house, especially an older one.
  • After a second or third clog in a short time.
  • Before a hydro jetting job on an old line.
  • When a floor drain backs up and the cause is unclear.
  • After a repair, to confirm the line is sound.

A pre-purchase inspection can reveal problems before closing, when they are easier to negotiate. See our hydro jetting page and sewer line repair page for what happens after.

What we do with the video

We review it with you on the spot. If the line is sound, we say so and you are done. If it shows damage, we explain the repair choices and the price range before anything is booked. You can keep the video file, which helps if you need to talk to a neighbor, the town or an insurer.

We do not guess at problems we have not seen, and we do not recommend digging when cleaning would do.
