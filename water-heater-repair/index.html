Water heater trouble in Chelmsford: leaks, cold water and strange noises

When the water heater fails in a Chelmsford house, the first question is whether it is leaking. A tank that is weeping from the bottom is usually finished. A tank that is dry but lukewarm is usually a part. Our emergency water heater repair line is open 24/7, and we ask the same questions every time so the right truck arrives with the right parts.

If water is on the floor, close the cold-water shutoff above the heater, switch off the gas or the breaker feeding it, and call. Do not open the drain valve on a hot tank without a hose and a floor drain. For no-hot-water calls, check that the breaker has not tripped and that a gas unit's pilot or igniter is lit before you call, because it is a quick check and sometimes the whole fix.

A repair is the right choice when the tank is sound and the part is cheap. When it is not, we tell you so and walk you through replacement options without pressure.

No hot water: is it an emergency?

No hot water in February in Massachusetts feels urgent, and for a family with young children or a person with medical needs it is. It is not an emergency in the safety sense. A leaking tank, a gas smell or scorching water at the tap is.

Gas smellLeave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us.
Water on the floorClose the cold supply to the heater and cut power to it.
Scalding waterA stuck thermostat or valve can overheat the tank. Turn the heater to its lowest setting and call.
Rumbling or poppingSediment on the tank bottom is the usual cause and it shortens the tank's life.

Common causes of a water heater that has stopped working

SymptomLikely causeTypical remedy.
Lukewarm water, electric tankFailed lower element or thermostatReplace the element or thermostat.
No hot water, gas tankDirty burner, failed thermocouple or igniterClean or replace the part.
Water at the baseCorroded tank or loose fittingTighten a fitting, otherwise replace the tank.
Pops and rumblesSediment layer on the tank floorFlush the tank, check the anode rod.
Hot water runs out fastBroken dip tube or sedimentReplace the tube, flush.

Rust-colored hot water points to the tank itself. If that water runs only from the hot side, the anode rod may be spent. Replacing the rod can stretch the life of a sound tank, which is a cheaper fix than a new unit.

When a repair makes sense and when it does not

A tank less than ten years old with a failed element or thermostat is nearly always worth repairing. A tank that leaks from the shell is not. Water heaters do not leak from the shell on purpose, and once the steel has corroded through, no patch holds.

If your unit is a tankless model, the usual issues are scale, a clogged inlet filter and an ignition fault, and the fix is a flush or a part. We service tankless systems and tell you when a flush costs less than a new unit.

Gas, electric and what we check on every call

  • The temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe, because it is a safety device.
  • Venting on gas units, since backdrafting can be a carbon monoxide risk.
  • The expansion tank or the lack of one on a closed system.
  • Sediment buildup and the condition of the anode rod.
  • The water supply valves and connectors, which are a common slow leak.
