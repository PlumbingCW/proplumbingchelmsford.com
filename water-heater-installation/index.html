Installing a water heater the planned way

Water heater installation in Chelmsford is easier when it is not an emergency. You can compare fuel types, check clearances and schedule the work around your week instead of the leak. We install standard tank heaters, tankless units and heat pump models, and the licensed plumber doing the work pulls the permit.

A good installation starts with the space. Gas units need a safe flue path and combustion air. Electric units need the right circuit. Heat pump units need open space and a condensate drain. We look at the closet, basement or garage before we recommend anything.

If the old heater has already failed, see our emergency water heater replacement page. This page is for the planned job.

Which type fits your home

TypeStrengthsWatch for.
Gas tankFast recovery, familiar serviceNeeds venting and a gas line.
Electric tankSimple installationHigher operating cost where electricity is pricey.
Heat pump tankEfficient, may qualify for rebatesNeeds space and a drain.
TanklessEndless hot water, small footprintHigher cost, annual flushing, gas sizing.

Utility and state incentives for heat pump units change often. Check current Mass Save terms before you buy.

What a code-compliant installation includes

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size discharge pipe.
  • New supply connectors and shutoff valves.
  • A thermal expansion tank where the system is closed.
  • Correct gas piping, venting and combustion air for gas units.
  • A drain pan or other protection where leaks would damage finished space.
  • A permit pulled by the licensed plumber and an inspection by the town.

Sizing: how big should the tank be

Size is based on how many people use hot water at once, how many bathrooms you have and whether the household runs the dishwasher and shower together. A tank too small runs out. A tank too large costs more to buy and to heat.

We ask about your morning routine because that is the peak. If you are comparing a tank to a tankless unit, flow rate matters more than gallons.

Installation day, step by step

  1. Pre-visit checksWe confirm the fuel, the space and the access for the new unit.
  2. Old unit outWe shut down, drain and remove the old heater.
  3. New unit inWe set, level, connect and vent the new unit.
  4. Test and walk-throughWe test for leaks and temperature and show you the shutoffs.

What the install day looks like

  1. Drain and disconnectWe shut off fuel and water, drain the old tank and disconnect the lines.
  2. Remove and haul awayThe old tank is removed from the house.
  3. Set and connect the new unitWe place the new heater, connect the water, and add an expansion tank when the system needs one.
  4. Gas or electric hookupWe connect and leak test the fuel, or wire the electrical connection.
  5. Fill, purge and testWe fill the tank, purge air and check every joint.

Most replacements finish in a single visit. A change of fuel or location takes longer.
