Hydro jetting in Chelmsford: when a snake is not enough

Hydro jetting uses a hose with a rotating nozzle and high-pressure water to scour the inside of a drain or sewer line. It clears grease, soap scum, sand and soft roots that a cable leaves clinging to the pipe wall. We offer it as an emergency service in Chelmsford, open 24/7, because the situations that call for it are usually backups.

If the line has already backed up into the house, stop using water, keep people clear of floor drains and call. A jetting visit will start with a camera so we know what is in the pipe, because jetting a collapsed or offset pipe can make the problem worse.

Jetting is not a cure for a broken pipe. It cleans what a pipe can still carry. When the camera shows a crack, a belly or a collapsed joint, we explain repair before any pressure goes into the line.

When a sewer backup is a jetting emergency

A main line that has backed up into a tub, a basement drain or a toilet is a health issue as well as a plumbing one. If the earlier snaking got stuck, only cleared a small hole or the line backed up again in weeks, jetting is usually the next step.

What to do before we jet your line

  • Tell us where the cleanout is and clear a path to it.
  • Keep children and pets away from the work area and the hose.
  • Do not run water in the house during the cleaning.
  • Close or cap low fixtures if we ask you to.
  • Move anything stored near the cleanout.

Where jetting works best in a house

Kitchen lines packed with grease are the classic case, and so are long sewer laterals with scale and root hair. Older cast iron can have rough interior surfaces that catch debris, which jetting reduces. Commercial kitchens use jetting on a schedule for the same reason.

If the line runs to the town sewer, the town's Sewer Division handles the public main and asks residents to call 978-250-5233 for sewer emergencies. We clear the section on your property and tell you if the problem is on the municipal side.

What happens on a jetting visit

  1. Camera inspectionWe look for cracks, offsets and roots before applying pressure.
  2. Access and setupWe use a cleanout, or pull a fixture if there is none.
  3. Jetting passThe nozzle cleans the line in stages, working from clean to dirty.
  4. Follow-up videoWe recheck with the camera and show you the cleaned line.

If the follow-up camera shows damage, we can talk through sewer line repair options with you.
