Replacing a failed water heater in Chelmsford

A water heater that has failed all at once is one of the most stressful plumbing calls a homeowner makes. The basement is wet, the family needs hot water, and the decision cannot wait. Our emergency water heater replacement service answers the 24/7 line, sends a plumber, and quotes the job before the old tank leaves its pan.

Before we arrive, close the cold-water shutoff, turn off the gas or the breaker, and move anything stored near the tank. Pull your phone out and photograph the label on the heater. The model, size and fuel type tell us what to bring.

Replacement is also a good time to fix whatever caused the failure. We look at the venting, the expansion tank and the supply connections, and the licensed plumber doing the work pulls the permit.

When a tank has failed and you need hot water today

A leaking tank is done. The question is how fast the replacement can be set. In most cases, a standard 40 or 50 gallon tank is something we can source for a same-day appointment when our schedule allows. Special venting, a different fuel or a tight space adds steps.

If the old unit is leaking onto finished flooring, shut off the supply and extract what water you can with towels. A wet-dry vacuum helps. Do not run the heater empty.

Choosing the right replacement

OptionBest forTrade-offs.
Standard tank, gasHomes already piped for gas with an existing flueLower upfront cost, needs proper venting.
Standard tank, electricHomes with no gas or a short flueSimple install, slower recovery.
TanklessSmaller spaces and steady hot waterHigher upfront, gas line and venting may need upgrades.
Heat pump tankLower running cost with a good spaceNeeds clearance and a drain, runs cooler air.

A switch of fuel or style is a bigger job than a like-for-like swap. We lay out the choices and the extras so the bill matches the estimate.

What the replacement visit includes

  1. Confirm size and fuelWe read the label and match the new tank to your household and your flue or circuit.
  2. Shut down and drainPower or gas goes off, the supply is closed and the old tank is drained.
  3. Install and connectWe set the new unit, replace supply connectors and add a code-compliant relief valve discharge.
  4. Test and start upWe check for leaks, light or energize the unit and verify temperature and pressure.
  5. Haul away and permitThe old tank is hauled away, and the permit is handled by the licensed plumber.

Replace or repair? An honest comparison

A tank that leaks from the shell should be replaced. A tank with a failed part and a sound shell can be repaired. If you are on the fence, see our water heater repair page for what that route looks like. When a tank is more than a decade old and you are paying for repairs more than once, replacement usually wins.

Replacing before it fails costs less than replacing in the middle of the night, and there is time to pick a better model. If you are still using an older unit, we are happy to schedule an assessment.
